# WealthAI Logic Explained (Simple Version)

## 📚 Overview
This document explains how the WealthAI system makes decisions, calculates costs, and tracks performance. We have removed complex math symbols to make it easy to understand for everyone.

## 1. 🎯 How We Pick Stocks (Momentum Strategy)
**Goal:** We want to buy assets that are already performing well (Winners).

### A. Finding the "Highs" and "Lows"
First, the system looks at the price history for the last **1 year (252 trading days)**.
*   **52-Week High:** The highest price the stock reached in the last year.
*   **52-Week Low:** The lowest price the stock dropped to in the last year.

### B. The "Momentum" Score
We rank stocks based on how close they are to their 1-year peak.
*   **Formula:** `(Current Price - 52Week High) / 52Week High`
*   **Example:**
    *   Stock A reached a high of ₹100.
    *   Current Price is ₹95.
    *   Calculation: (95 - 100) / 100 = **-5%** (It is very close to its peak).
    *   *The closer this number is to 0%, the stronger the momentum.*

### C. Increasing Investment (Churning)
If the portfolio is making a profit, we slowly increase the amount of money we trade each week.
*   **Logic:** For every **20% profit** the portfolio makes, we increase our weekly trading amount by **10%**.
*   **Why?** This helps us "compound" our gains—using profits to make potentially bigger profits.

## 2. 🚀 How We Rank Stocks (Relative Strength)
**Goal:** We want to own stocks that are beating the market (Nifty 50).

### A. The "RS Score"
We don't just look at if a stock went up; we look at if it went up **more than the market**. We check this over three time periods:
1.  **1 Week** (Short term)
2.  **1 Month** (Medium term)
3.  **1 Quarter** (Long term)

### B. The Final Score
We take the average of those three periods.
*   **Formula:** `(Week Score + Month Score + Quarter Score) / 3`
*   **Result:** A single number that tells us if this stock is a consistent winner against the market.

### C. Position Sizing (Don't put all eggs in one basket)
We split our money equally among a set number of stocks (e.g., 20 stocks).
*   **Formula:** `Total Money / 20 Stocks`
*   **Example:** If you have ₹10,00,000, we buy ₹50,000 worth of each stock.

## 3. 💸 Costs & Fees (Indian Market)
We calculate costs exactly like a real broker (Zerodha/Upstox) to make the simulation realistic.

### A. Buying Costs
When we **BUY** a stock, we pay:
1.  **Brokerage:** 0.1% of the trade value.
2.  **Stamp Duty:** 0.005% (Government tax).
3.  **Exchange Fees:** 0.00297% (Paid to NSE).
4.  **SEBI Fees:** 0.0001% (Regulatory fee).
5.  **GST:** 18% tax on the Brokerage + Exchange Fees + SEBI Fees.

### B. Selling Costs
When we **SELL** a stock, we pay all the above, **PLUS**:
1.  **STT (Securities Transaction Tax):** 0.1% of the total value.

**Summary:** We deduct roughly **0.1% to 0.2%** from every trade to account for these real-world costs.

## 4. 🏛️ Taxes (Capital Gains)
We follow the "First-In-First-Out" (FIFO) rule used by the Income Tax Department.

### A. FIFO Method
Imagine you bought shares of Tata Motors three times:
1.  Jan: Bought 10 shares
2.  Feb: Bought 10 shares
3.  Mar: Bought 10 shares

If you sell **15 shares** today, the system assumes:
*   You sold all 10 from **Jan** (First In).
*   You sold 5 from **Feb**.
*   You still keep the ones from Mar.

### B. The Tax Rate
*   We calculate the profit on the specific shares sold.
*   **Tax Rate:** We deduct **12.5%** of the profit as Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax.

## 5. 📈 Measuring Success (Performance Metrics)
How do we know if the strategy is good?

1.  **CAGR (Annual Growth):**
    *   What is the average return per year? (e.g., 25% per year).

2.  **Max Drawdown (Risk):**
    *   What was the *worst* drop from the peak?
    *   **Example:** If the portfolio went from ₹1,00,000 to ₹1,50,000, but then dropped to ₹1,20,000 before going back up, the "Drawdown" is that drop from 150k to 120k (-20%).
    *   *Lower is better.*

3.  **Win Rate:**
    *   Out of 100 trades, how many were profitable? (e.g., 60% win rate).
